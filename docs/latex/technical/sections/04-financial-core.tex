\subsection{Estimating the model (stage 1)}

\code{estimate_model} turns a ledger of transactions into a \code{FinancialModel}, over pay cycles rather than calendar
months, and refuses fewer than six cycles of history. In short (\code{docs/FINANCIAL_CORE.md} \S4.1--4.3):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{commitments}: cuotas from structured metadata or from \code{CUOTA nn/NN} text, and recurring payments
    with a 28--35-day cadence and amounts within 5\%;
  \item \emph{income}: the contracted net income, lowered to the recent median when the two differ by more than 5\%;
    for freelance income, the P50 (typical) and P20 (conservative) of cycle totals;
  \item \emph{variable spend}: P50 and P80 per category, by linear interpolation on exact fractions, net of refunds
    and excluding cuotas, recurring payments and own transfers; sparse categories merge into \code{other}; one
    December factor, clamped to $[1, 2]$.
\end{itemize}
The estimators trust the ledger's \code{transfer_own} label (ADR-012), which is a known risk for real data.

\subsection{Forecast at pay-cycle troughs}\label{sec:forecast}

Liquidity is checked at each cycle's \emph{trough}: the balance just before salary arrives. With $B_0$ the opening
trough, for each projected cycle $m$:
\begin{equation}
  B_m = B_{m-1} + I_m - K_m - V_m - R_m - P_m - S_m, \qquad H_m = B_m - F,
\end{equation}
where $I$ is income, $K$ commitments due, $V$ variable spend, $R$ a savings rule (the plan), $P$ purchase installments
and $S$ a shock. A cycle \emph{breaches} when $H_m < 0$. The \emph{typical} scenario uses typical income and P50 spend;
\emph{conservative}, used for every decision, uses conservative income and P80 spend; \emph{stress} is conservative
plus one shock $\max(300{,}000, \text{largest one-off expense})$ in the first cycle. The lowest headroom, ties broken
by the earliest month, is the binding month (\code{project} in \code{forecast.py}, \cref{alg:project}).

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Projection (\texttt{project}, \texttt{domain/finance/forecast.py}).}\label{alg:project}
  \KwIn{model $M$, parameters, scenario $\in$ \{typical, conservative, stress\}, horizon $h \geq 1$, optional plan, purchases, shock}
  \KwOut{cycles with trough $B_m$ and headroom $H_m$}
  \lIf{scenario is stress and no shock given}{shock $\gets \max(\mathit{stress\_shock\_min}, M.\mathit{largest\_one\_off})$ in $M.\mathit{first\_cycle}$}
  $q \gets$ P50 if typical else P80;\quad $I \gets$ typical income if typical else conservative income\;
  $B \gets M.\mathit{balances.checking}$\;
  \For{$m \gets M.\mathit{first\_cycle}$ \KwTo $M.\mathit{first\_cycle} + h - 1$}{
    $A_m \gets I - M.\mathit{commitments\_due}(m) - M.\mathit{spend}(m, q)$ \Comment*[r]{available}
    $B \gets B + A_m - \mathit{plan}(m) - \sum \mathit{purchases}(m) - \mathit{shock}(m)$\;
    record $(m, B, H_m = B - F)$\;
  }
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Protected surplus}\label{sec:surplus}

The protected surplus over a horizon $h$ is the lowest conservative headroom, with the plan and any purchases included
(\code{protected_surplus} in \code{surplus.py}):
\begin{equation}
  \mathit{PS}(h) = \min_{m \leq h} \Bigl[ B_0 - F + \sum_{k \leq m} (A_k - R_k - P_k) \Bigr].
\end{equation}
It is how much could leave checking today without any conservative trough falling below the floor. A negative value is
a breach, not a debt; the engine never calls a positive value ``safe''.

\subsection{Financial capacity}\label{sec:capacity}

Capacity (\code{assess_capacity} in \code{capacity.py}) bands five factors and takes the weakest. Essentials are the
P80 of essential categories (groceries, transport, utilities, health) without seasonality, plus average rent and
average debt service over the six-month dashboard horizon. With zero essentials every ratio is undefined, so the engine
raises \code{InsufficientData} (hard rule H4) instead of dividing by zero.

The weakest band maps to a profile (low $\to$ conservative, medium $\to$ moderate, high $\to$ aggressive), and every
factor tied at that band is reported as binding. The \emph{permitted profile} is the lower of the declared tolerance
(from the questionnaire) and capacity (\code{derive_permitted_profile}); the plan use case recomputes it server-side, so
a request for a higher profile is capped and flagged \code{PROFILE_CAPPED}. Language never raises a declared tolerance;
without one the tolerance is conservative, or at most moderate when the decision model reads a confident signal.

\subsection{Sizing a savings plan}\label{sec:plan}

\Cref{alg:plan} is \code{size_plan} in \code{plan.py}. It searches the earliest start month whose rule is positive and
keeps the conservative path above the floor. The rule is a share of the lowest available amount in a six-month window,
capped by what goals and the reserve top-up actually need, and rounded down to 10{,}000.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Plan sizing (\texttt{size\_plan}).}\label{alg:plan}
  \KwIn{model $M$, parameters, permitted profile $p$, purchases}
  \KwOut{a rule amount and start month, its allocation, and the hard rules triggered}
  $E \gets$ essentials;\quad $\mathit{target} \gets 3E$;\quad $\mathit{min} \gets E$;\quad $s_p \gets \sigma(p)$\;
  $G \gets$ goals with a deadline within 12 months, by deadline\;
  $A \gets$ conservative available minus purchases, per cycle, over $C + W$ cycles\;
  \For{$i \gets 0$ \KwTo $C - 1$}{
    $s \gets M.\mathit{first\_cycle} + i$;\quad $\ell \gets \min_{k < W} A_{s+k}$\;
    \lIf{$\ell \leq 0$}{\KwContinue}
    $\mathit{required} \gets \mathrm{round}\bigl(\max(0, \mathit{target} - \mathit{reserve}) / 24\bigr) + \sum_{g \in G} \lceil (g.\mathit{target} - g.\mathit{saved}) / \max(1, n_{s,g}) \rceil$ \Comment*[r]{$n_{s,g}$: cycles from $s$ to $g$'s deadline}
    $R \gets \mathrm{floor}_{10{,}000}\bigl(\min(s_p \cdot \ell, \mathit{required})\bigr)$\;
    \lIf{$R \leq 0$}{\KwContinue}
    \lIf{the conservative projection with rule $R$ from $s$ breaches before $s + C$}{\KwContinue \Comment*[r]{H5}}
    \eIf{$M.\mathit{reserve} < \mathit{min}$}{\Return $R$, $s$, all to the reserve, H1}{
      give each goal in $G$ $\min(\text{remaining}, \text{its need})$; the rest to the reserve\;
      \Return $R$, $s$, allocation}
  }
  \Return no rule, H2\;
\end{algorithm}

The plan's envelope also says why the plan suits the user (UG01). It repeats the permitted-profile facts
(\code{profile.permitted}, \code{profile.tolerance}, \code{profile.capacity}) and adds the capacity level and its
binding factor. With Camila's fixture, an aggressive tolerance meets conservative capacity, bound by conservative
headroom: the permitted share is $\tfrac25$. Her conservative available amount is negative in November and December
2026, so the first window that passes starts in January 2027. Its lowest month is 260{,}000, so the rule is
$\mathrm{floor}_{10{,}000}(\tfrac25 \cdot 260{,}000) = 100{,}000$ per month. All of it goes to her trip goal, which is
on track for September 2027.

The search is not monotone: a higher profile, or an extra purchase, can produce a smaller rule.
\code{tests/property/test_plan_counterexamples.py} keeps two minimal counterexamples as strict expected failures, and
ADR-014 proposes a fix that is not accepted or built.

\subsection{Simulating a purchase}\label{sec:purchase}

A purchase of principal $P$ in $n$ equal cuotas at monthly rate $r$ costs, per cuota (\code{installments.py}),
\begin{equation}
  c = \operatorname{round}\Bigl(P \cdot \frac{r}{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}\Bigr) \quad (r > 0), \qquad c = \operatorname{round}(P / n) \quad (r = 0),
\end{equation}
with total cost $n \cdot c$; one cuota carries no interest whatever the rate. \code{simulate_purchase} overlays the
cuotas on the typical and conservative projections over $\max(C, \text{months to the last cuota})$ and returns a
verdict: \code{BASELINE_BREACH} when the conservative path already breaches without the purchase, \code{FITS_ALL}
when the conservative path holds with it, \code{FITS_TYPICAL_ONLY} when only the typical path holds, else
\code{BREACHES_ALL}. In the last two cases it offers up to three deterministic alternatives (defer the start, another
cuota count, a smaller amount), each re-simulated. The largest affordable cuota accounts for cuotas accumulating in
the trough: with $k_m$ the number of cuotas paid by cycle $m$,
\begin{equation}
  c_{\max} = \min_{m \geq \text{first cuota}} \left\lfloor H_m / k_m \right\rfloor .
\end{equation}

\subsection{The spending check-in}\label{sec:spending}

\code{domain/finance/spending.py} (ADR-039) compares what the user has spent in the current pay cycle with what the
conservative forecast already assumes for it. Logged spending is the statement charges plus the verified receipts
and confirmed expenses that no charge has recorded yet. With $V^{80}$ the cycle's P80 variable spending (seasonal
factor applied) and $L$ the logged spending,
\begin{equation}
  \mathit{left} = V^{80} - L, \qquad \mathit{per\_day} = \Bigl\lfloor \frac{\max(0, \mathit{left})}{\text{days left in the cycle}} \Bigr\rfloor, \qquad \mathit{surplus\_after} = \mathit{PS} - \max(0, -\mathit{left}).
\end{equation}
Overspending in a projected cycle lowers every later trough by the same amount, so it comes out of the protected
surplus one to one. If today's cycle comes before the forecast's first one, overspending only produces a warning. The
T0 tool \code{get_spending_status} returns these figures as facts, with \code{SPENDING_ABOVE_FORECAST} or
\code{SPENDING_BREACHES_FLOOR} when they apply.

\subsection{Importing a statement}\label{sec:import}

\Cref{fig:dataflow} is the import path (\code{application/use_cases/statements.py}). The reader routes a file by its
bytes, not its declared type: an Excel export is parsed with the standard library; a PDF uses its text layer, and a scan
or photo needs OCR. Redaction runs right after reading, before anything is stored, classified, audited or returned
(ADR-028): deterministic rules in \code{domain/privacy/redaction.py} replace RUTs (checked with modulus 11), runs of
seven or more digits, emails, Chilean phones and the counterparty of a transfer with placeholders such as
\code{[PERSON]} and \code{[ID]}, and keep only the last four digits of an account. An optional detector can only add
masking. If any statement in a file cannot be redacted, nothing from the file is stored.

  \centering

  \caption{Statement upload. Every payload is sealed before it reaches SQLite; the audit records counts, never text.}\label{fig:dataflow}

Reconciliation is the authority over the reader (ADR-020): \code{reconcile} recomputes totals, section totals, the
closing balance and every printed daily balance from the lines, so a misread amount, a dropped line or a wrong day
breaks an equality. A statement that does not reconcile is stored as unverified, with a warning naming each issue, and
is not posted. A verified, final statement is posted (\code{post_statement}): on a checking account deposits are income
by rule and charges are purchases; a classifier's category is accepted only if it is in the vocabulary with a
confidence of at least 0.5, otherwise the charge is \code{uncategorized}. A category the user sets wins over any model
(ADR-023).

\subsection{The response verifier}\label{sec:verifier}

\code{verify_explanation} (\code{domain/agent/verification.py}) checks a reply against the facts and warnings of the
tools called in the same turn (\cref{alg:verify}). A failed reply is replaced by \code{narration}, a deterministic text
built from the same facts, and the failure is audited as \code{VERIFICATION_FAILED}. The same check covers the
newer read tools. A concept card declares every number its body states, and the envelope turns each into a fact. Its
\code{NOT_ADVICE} warning must be mentioned (ADR-040). Figures from investments are facts with the warning
\code{INVESTMENTS_AS_REPORTED}, or \code{INVESTMENTS_FROM_STATEMENT} for imported documents. A connected service's
numbers are facts in the currency their object names, or else in the unit \code{external}, which the verifier never
accepts as money. They carry the warning \code{EXTERNAL_DATA}.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Reply verification (\texttt{verify\_explanation}).}\label{alg:verify}
  \KwIn{reply text $x$, facts $\Phi$, warnings $\Omega$, verdicts and execution results of the turn}
  \KwOut{pass, or the list of issues}
  \ForEach{number $v$ in $x$ (currency prefixes, thousands separators, k/mil/M suffixes, percentages)}{
    \If{$v$ matches no fact exactly, nor to the nearest 1{,}000 after ``about'', nor a percentage fact}{issue \code{UNSUPPORTED_NUMBER}}}
  \ForEach{warning $w \in \Omega$}{\lIf{no token of $w.\mathit{must\_mention}$ occurs in $x$}{issue \code{MISSING_WARNING}}}
  \lIf{$x$ says ``safe money'', ``safe to spend'' or ``guaranteed'' (English or Spanish)}{issue \code{BANNED_WORDING}}
  \lIf{a verdict is not \code{FITS_ALL} and $x$ affirms the purchase}{issue \code{CONTRADICTION}}
  \lIf{$x$ claims a rule was created or executed, or that the agent itself moved, transferred, deposited or invested money, and no execution succeeded}{issue \code{FALSE_EXECUTION_CLAIM}}
  \Return pass if there is no issue\;
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{The audit chain and erasure}\label{sec:chain}

The audit log is one hash chain for all users (ADR-008). An event's hash covers every field of its record and the
previous event's hash, so editing, inserting or deleting an event is detected by \code{verify_chain}. Account deletion
(\cref{sec:deletion}) must remove a user's events without breaking the chain for everyone else. Each of the user's
events becomes a \emph{tombstone}: it keeps its sequence, \code{prev_hash} and \code{event_hash}, so the links still
verify, and loses everything else (kind \code{ERASED}, actor system, no user, session \code{erased}). A tombstone can no
longer be re-hashed, so it is accepted only when a later erasure event commits to it (\cref{fig:chain},
\cref{alg:chain}).

  \centering

  \caption{The chain after user A's account is deleted.}\label{fig:chain}
\end{figure}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
  \caption{Chain verification with tombstones (\texttt{verify\_chain}, \texttt{domain/audit/events.py}).}\label{alg:chain}
  \KwIn{events $e_1, \dots, e_n$ by sequence}
  \KwOut{ok, or the first broken sequence and a reason}
  $\mathit{prev} \gets 0^{64}$;\quad $T \gets \{\}$ (erasure id $\to$ tombstone sequences);\quad $X \gets \{\}$ (erasure events)\;
  \For{$i \gets 1$ \KwTo $n$}{
    \lIf{$e_i.\mathit{sequence} \neq i$}{\KwFail ``sequence gap''}
    \lIf{$e_i.\mathit{prev\_hash} \neq \mathit{prev}$}{\KwFail ``prev\_hash does not link''}
    \eIf{$e_i$ is a tombstone naming erasure $j$}{
      \lIf{$j \in X$}{\KwFail ``erased after its erasure event''}
      append $i$ to $T[j]$\;
    }{\lIf{$\mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{canonical}(e_i)) \neq e_i.\mathit{event\_hash}$}{\KwFail ``event content changed''}}
    \lIf{$e_i$ is \code{ACCOUNT_DELETED} or \code{DEMO_DATA_RESET}}{$X[e_i.\mathit{id}] \gets e_i$}
    $\mathit{prev} \gets e_i.\mathit{event\_hash}$\;
  }
  \ForEach{$(j, S) \in T$}{\lIf{$j \notin X$ or $X[j]$ does not commit to $(|S|, \mathrm{SHA256}(S))$}{\KwFail ``erasure not recorded''}}
  \ForEach{$(j, x) \in X$}{\lIf{$x$ commits to a count other than $|T[j]|$}{\KwFail ``erasure event does not match''}}
  \Return ok\;
\end{algorithm}

The hash is computed over the plaintext record, exactly as \code{neural-ledger export-audit} writes it, so an export
verifies without any key. In the database the whole record, hashes included, is sealed (\cref{sec:at-rest}).
