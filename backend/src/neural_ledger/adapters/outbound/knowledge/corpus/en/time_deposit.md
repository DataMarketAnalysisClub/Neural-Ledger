A time deposit is money placed with a bank for a fixed period that ends on a set maturity date, usually in exchange for interest agreed at the start. In Chile it is called depósito a plazo. It can be fixed, meaning the amount and term are set when you open it, or renewable, meaning it is placed again at maturity depending on what the contract says. Some banks also offer deposits denominated in UF, whose value is readjusted with the UF; check the contract to see which currency applies.

Because the money is committed for the whole term, check whether you will need it before maturity. Early withdrawal usually carries a penalty or a lower interest payment, and the deposit normally cannot be transferred to someone else.

Before deciding, look at the term, the rate, when interest is paid, whether the deposit renews automatically, and its currency. Those conditions appear in the contract. They vary between banks and change over time, so compare them directly with each institution and on official sources.

Money that earns interest in a deposit is also exposed to inflation, which is why the real versus nominal card matters here.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
