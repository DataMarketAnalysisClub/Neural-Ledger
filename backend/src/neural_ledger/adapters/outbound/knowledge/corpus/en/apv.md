Voluntary pension savings, known in Chile as Ahorro Previsional Voluntario or APV, is an optional way to save for retirement on top of the mandatory pension contributions. The money sits in a dedicated account, offered by institutions authorized for this purpose, and it is meant to fund a pension.

APV is tied to tax rules. Under regime A, contributions reduce the taxable income of the year they are made, and the amounts withdrawn are taxed when they are received. Under regime B, contributions come from income that has already been taxed, and withdrawals are generally not taxed again. Which regime fits depends on each person's situation, and the tax rules themselves can change.

The main risks are that the savings are invested and their value can fall, that the money is usually locked until retirement or until a set condition is met, and that the tax benefit depends on personal circumstances and on the law in force. Fees charged by the institution also reduce the balance.

Before deciding anything, check the current rules with the Superintendencia de Pensiones and the CMF, and read the contract of the institution that would hold the account.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
