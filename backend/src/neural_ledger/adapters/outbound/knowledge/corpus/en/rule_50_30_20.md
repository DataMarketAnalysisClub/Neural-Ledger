The 50/30/20 rule is a budgeting guideline that splits your monthly take-home pay into three buckets: about 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for saving or paying down debt. It is a simple starting point for people who do not want to track every peso.

Needs are costs you cannot easily skip, such as rent or a mortgage, food, transport, utilities and insurance. Wants are optional, like dining out, streaming services, trips and new clothes. The last bucket moves you forward: an emergency fund, a savings goal, or paying off high-interest debt.

In Chile the split often needs adjusting. In Santiago, rent alone can take a large part of a salary, so a household might use something like 60/20/20 and trim its wants. If your income varies, base the percentages on an average of several months, and set the savings transfer for payday so the money is set aside before you spend it.

The rule is a guideline, not a law or a fixed formula. Use it to see where your money goes, then adjust the percentages to your own situation.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
