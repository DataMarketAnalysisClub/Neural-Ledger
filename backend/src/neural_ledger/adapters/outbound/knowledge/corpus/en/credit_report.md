A credit report is a record of how a person has handled their debts, built from information that banks, card issuers and other creditors send to a reporting agency. In Chile it is usually called an informe comercial or boletín comercial, and in everyday speech people often say Dicom.

Lenders check it when someone applies for a loan, a card or a line of credit. It shows open debts, payment history, late payments and unpaid obligations that went to collection. Lenders use it to judge how likely a person is to pay, and that judgment shapes the terms they offer.

An old problem weighs less than a recent one, and each entry carries a date that can be checked.

You can look at what is recorded about you. If an entry is wrong, it is worth raising it with the company that reported it. For general questions about credit, the CMF publishes useful information.

When reviewing it, it also helps to look for accounts, inquiries or addresses you do not recognise, since they can point to identity theft. An error is easier to correct before you apply for credit than during a lender's review.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
