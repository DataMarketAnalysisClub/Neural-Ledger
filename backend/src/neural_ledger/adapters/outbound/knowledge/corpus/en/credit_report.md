A credit report is a record of how a person has handled their debts, built from information that banks, card issuers and other creditors send to a reporting agency. In Chile it is usually called an informe comercial or boletín comercial, and in everyday speech people often say Dicom.

Lenders check it when someone applies for a loan, a card or a line of credit. It shows open debts, payment history, late payments and unpaid obligations that went to collection. Lenders use it to judge how likely a person is to pay, and that judgment shapes the terms they offer.

Negative records stay on the report for a period that the law sets. An old problem fades over time, but it does not disappear at once, and each entry carries a date you can check.

You have the right to know what is recorded about you. You can request a copy of your own information, review it for mistakes and ask for corrections when an entry is wrong. If a dispute is not resolved, consumer complaints can go to SERNAC, and the CMF oversees the banks and financial institutions that report information.

Reviewing the report before you apply for credit is a sensible habit. An error you fix early is much easier to correct than one a lender finds first.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
