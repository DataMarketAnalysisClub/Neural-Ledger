The Carga Anual Equivalente (CAE) is Chile's all-in annual cost of a credit, shown as a percentage, that combines the interest with the other charges attached to the loan.

A rate on its own shows only part of the cost. A credit can carry a modest interest rate and still be expensive once commissions and administrative fees are added. The CAE gathers those costs into one figure, so offers from different institutions can be placed side by side. Chilean law requires lenders to show it in advertising that displays a payment or reference rate, and quotes must detail prices, charges and commissions.

The comparison only works under the same conditions. Two credits are comparable when they have the same amount and the same term, because both factors change how much interest gets paid. A lower CAE on a credit with a different amount or length says little about which one costs less for you.

The CAE is a percentage, not a monthly payment. To know what you will pay each month, the payment table in the contract is the reference. It is worth confirming with the institution which costs the figure includes, since the CMF sets the rules for how it is calculated.

The US annual percentage rate (APR), which is often presented as the closest equivalent, is calculated under different rules and does not always include every charge. For that reason the two figures are not interchangeable.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
