A cryptocurrency is a digital asset whose ownership records are kept on a distributed ledger, usually a blockchain, which many computers maintain together instead of a single bank or institution. Transfers are recorded and verified through a process run by the network.

In Chile, the Central Bank does not treat cryptocurrencies as legal tender, so nobody is obliged to accept them as payment. They are also not covered the way bank deposits are, so if a platform where they are held fails or is hacked, the money may not be recovered. The rules for these assets have changed in recent years and may change again, and other countries treat them differently.

The central risk is volatility. Prices can move sharply within days or hours, and some coins have lost most of their value in a short time. Other risks include scams, failed or hacked platforms, stablecoins that did not keep their stated value, and losing access to a wallet or its keys.

For these reasons, cryptocurrencies are usually grouped with the highest-risk assets, and the amount put in may not come back. Understanding how a given asset works and where it is held is a sensible first step before judging it.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
