A budget is a plan that compares the money coming into your account with the money going out, so you decide in advance how it will be used. It is not a punishment or a diary of regrets. It is a map for the coming month.

The usual first step is to start from what actually arrives. Use your take-home pay, not your gross salary, because budgeting from the gross figure overstates what you have available. If your income changes from month to month, average the last few months instead of relying on one good payday.

Next, sort your costs into fixed ones, which are hard to change, and variable ones, which you can adjust. Do not forget irregular costs such as a car inspection, a dentist visit or gifts, because they break plans when they are not expected. Setting aside a small amount each month for them keeps them manageable.

Then track real spending for at least one full month. Estimates tend to miss small daily purchases and forgotten subscriptions. Once you know the figures, assign an amount to each category and decide what is left for saving or paying debts. A budget is only useful when you review it and adjust it.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
