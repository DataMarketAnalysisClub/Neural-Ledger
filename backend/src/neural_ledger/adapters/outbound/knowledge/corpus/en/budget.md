A budget is a plan that compares the money coming into your account with the money going out, so you decide in advance how it will be used. It is not a punishment or a diary of regrets; it is a map for the coming month.

The process starts with the amount that really arrives. Working from net pay rather than the gross salary keeps the figures realistic. When pay changes from month to month, a multi-month average gives a steadier base than the best month.

Expenses are then split into fixed ones, which are hard to change, and variable ones, which can be adjusted. Irregular costs such as a car inspection, a dentist visit or gifts also need a place in the plan, because unplanned ones tend to break it. A small monthly reserve for them keeps those costs from becoming a shock.

A budget can live on paper, in a spreadsheet or in an app, but it works best when it is checked often. Writing down every expense over a complete month shows real spending, since estimates usually leave out small everyday purchases and subscriptions that were forgotten. With those figures, an amount is assigned to each category and the remainder is set for saving or paying debts. A budget is only useful when it is reviewed and adjusted.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
