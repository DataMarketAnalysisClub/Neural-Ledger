Liquidity is how quickly and easily an asset can be turned into spendable money without losing much of its value. Cash is the most liquid asset because it is already money, while a house or a piece of art takes time to sell and may need a lower price to find a buyer.

Liquidity has two parts: how fast you can convert the asset, and how much value you lose along the way. If a quick sale means a large discount, the asset is not really liquid, even if someone would buy it.

For a person, liquidity is about being able to pay expenses when they fall due. A large net worth on paper does not help much if most of it is tied up in things you cannot sell quickly. That is why budgeting and emergency savings focus on money you can reach on short notice.

The word also describes markets. A market with many trades lets you buy and sell at a price close to the current one.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
