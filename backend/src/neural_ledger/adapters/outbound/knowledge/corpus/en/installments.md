Buying in installments, or cuotas, means paying the price of a purchase in a fixed number of scheduled payments instead of all at once.

Installments come in two common forms. Some purchases are split with interest, so the total you pay is higher than the cash price. Others are advertised as cuotas sin interés, meaning no interest is added in the usual way. Even then, the installment price can differ from the cash price, so it is worth adding up every installment and comparing the sum with the cash price.

Each installment is a commitment. Once you sign, that amount leaves your account every month until the purchase is paid off. Installments from different purchases stack up, and the monthly total can be much larger than any single payment suggests. This is why they reduce the money you can spend freely in later months.

The cost of credit does not always show up in one place. When a lender reports the CAE, it gives an annual figure that includes the charges, which helps when comparing financing options. It is useful to ask for the full payment schedule, the total amount and whether early payment is allowed, since some contracts charge a penalty for paying ahead of time.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
