Buying in installments, or cuotas, means paying the price of a purchase in a fixed number of scheduled payments instead of all at once.

Installments come in two common forms. Some purchases are split with interest, so the total you pay is higher than the cash price. Others are advertised as cuotas sin interés, meaning no interest is added in the usual way. Even then, check whether the installment price matches the cash price, because the two can differ. Compare the cash price with the sum of every installment.

Each installment is a commitment. Once you sign, that amount leaves your account every month until the purchase is paid off. Installments from different purchases stack up, and the monthly total can be much larger than any single payment suggests. This is why they reduce the money you can spend freely in later months.

The cost of credit does not always show up in one place. When a lender reports the CAE, it gives an annual figure that includes the charges, which helps when comparing financing options. Ask the seller or bank for the full schedule, the total amount and whether early payment is allowed.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
