A savings goal is a specific amount of money you want to have by a specific date, for a clear reason. Naming the purpose, the amount and the deadline turns a vague wish to save into a plan you can check each month.

Start by setting the amount, which is the total cost of what you want, and the timeframe. Short-term goals take one year or less, medium-term goals take between one and five years, and long-term goals take longer. Then divide the total by the number of months. For example, $600.000 over 6 months means setting aside $100.000 each month.

Check that monthly amount against your budget before you commit. If it does not fit, you can lengthen the deadline, lower the target or split a large goal into smaller steps that show progress. Revisit the goal when your income or costs change, because a fixed plan can become unrealistic.

Keep goals separate from your emergency fund, which covers unexpected costs rather than planned purchases.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
