A savings goal is a specific amount of money you want to have by a specific date, for a clear reason. Naming the purpose, the amount and the deadline turns a vague wish to save into a plan you can check each month.

A common method is SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, realistic and time-bound. Begin by setting the amount, which is the total cost of what you want, and the timeframe. Goals due within a year are short-term, those due in one to five years are medium-term, and anything further out is long-term. Then divide the total by the number of months: $600.000 over 6 months means setting aside $100.000 each month.

Before committing, compare that monthly amount with your budget. If it does not fit, the deadline can be extended, the target lowered or the goal broken into smaller steps that show progress. A goal can also be revisited when income or costs change, since a fixed plan may stop being realistic.

Goals are kept separate from the emergency fund, which covers unexpected costs rather than planned purchases.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
