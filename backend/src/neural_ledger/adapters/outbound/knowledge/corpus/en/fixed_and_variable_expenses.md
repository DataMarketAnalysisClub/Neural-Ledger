Fixed expenses are costs that repeat at about the same amount each month and are hard to skip, such as rent or a mortgage payment, loan installments, school fees, insurance and basic health costs. Variable expenses change from month to month, and many can be postponed or reduced, such as groceries, transport, dining out, streaming subscriptions and new clothes.

Splitting costs this way shows where there is room to adjust. Fixed costs usually come first when a month is planned, since they recur on a schedule that is hard to change. Most of the possible adjustments sit in variable costs, because the person chooses how much to spend on them.

A simple test helps: ask whether the expense could be skipped for a while. If it is needed to live, to stay healthy or to keep a home, it is fixed. If it could be delayed or cancelled, it is variable.

Irregular costs, such as a car inspection or a yearly gift, fit neither group neatly. One way to handle them is to work out their total for the year and spread that amount across monthly savings payments.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
