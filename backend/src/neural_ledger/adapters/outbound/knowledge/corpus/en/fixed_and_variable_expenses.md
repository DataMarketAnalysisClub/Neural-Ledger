Fixed expenses are costs that repeat at about the same amount each month and are hard to skip, such as rent or a mortgage payment, loan installments, school fees, insurance and basic health costs. Variable expenses change from month to month, and many of them can be postponed or reduced, such as groceries, transport, dining out, streaming subscriptions and new clothes.

Splitting costs this way shows where you have room to adjust. Fixed costs are commitments you must cover first, so they are usually set aside before anything else in a budget. Variable costs are where most changes happen, because you decide how much to spend on them.

A simple test helps. Ask yourself whether you could go without the expense for a while. If you need it to live, to stay healthy or to keep your home, it is fixed. If you could delay or cancel it, it is variable.

Irregular costs, like a car inspection or a yearly gift, do not fit neatly in either group. Estimate them over the whole year and set aside a small amount each month so they do not catch you off guard.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
