The debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is the share of your gross income that goes to debt payments in a month.

To calculate it, add up the monthly payments on your debts, such as loan installments, card minimums and other recurring obligations. Divide that total by your gross monthly income, which is the amount before taxes and deductions, and express the result as a percentage.

Lenders use the ratio to judge whether a new payment fits within what you earn. Some models split it in two: housing costs on their own, and all recurring debts together. Chilean banks use similar measures, often called carga financiera, that compare cuotas with income.

The ratio has clear limits. Because it uses gross income, the money you actually take home is smaller, so your real margin is tighter than the figure suggests. It also ignores irregular spending, savings goals and emergencies. A low ratio does not mean the leftover money is free to spend.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
