The debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is the share of your gross monthly income that goes to debt payments each month.

To calculate it, add up the monthly payments on your debts, such as loan installments, card minimums and other recurring obligations. Divide that total by your gross monthly income, which is the amount before taxes and deductions, and express the result as a percentage. For example, with debt payments of 1,500,000 CLP a month and gross income of 5,000,000 CLP, the ratio would be 30%.

Lenders use the ratio to judge whether a new payment fits within what you earn. Some models split it in two: housing costs on their own, and all recurring debts together. Lenders also tend to look at payment history and job stability alongside this figure.

The ratio has clear limits. Because it uses gross income, the money you actually take home is smaller, so your real margin is tighter than the figure suggests. It also ignores irregular spending, savings goals and emergencies. A low ratio does not mean the leftover money is free to spend. Each institution in Chile sets its own criteria, so the formula here is the general version.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
