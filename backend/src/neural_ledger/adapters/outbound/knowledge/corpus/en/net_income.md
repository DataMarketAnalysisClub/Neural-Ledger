Net income is the part of your salary that actually reaches your bank account after legal deductions. In Chile it is called sueldo líquido, and it is the figure to use when you plan a budget.

Gross income, or sueldo bruto, is the amount written in your contract. From it, the employer deducts your mandatory pension contribution, which goes to your personal account at an AFP, your health contribution, which goes to Fonasa or an Isapre, and unemployment insurance, which applies to most indefinite contracts. The single income tax (impuesto único de segunda categoría) is then calculated on what remains, using progressive rates. Some allowances sit outside the taxable base and arrive in full.

The gap between gross and net can be large, so budgeting from the gross figure overstates the money you have. Rates, bases and tax bands are adjusted over time, so check current values with the Superintendencia de Pensiones and the SII rather than relying on a rule of thumb. Your payslip (liquidación de sueldo) lists each deduction and is the best source for your own case.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
