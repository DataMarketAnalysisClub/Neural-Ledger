Revolving credit is a credit line you can borrow from, repay and borrow from again, up to a set limit, without a fixed schedule of payments. The credit card is the most common example in Chile.

The issuer sets your limit based on its assessment of what you can repay. Every purchase or cash advance uses part of that limit, and each payment frees it up again. Your available credit therefore goes up and down with your balance.

Interest applies to the amount you have not paid. If you clear the full balance before the due date, you usually avoid interest on those purchases. If you pay less, the unpaid part carries interest, and that interest is added to what you owe, so the balance can grow even while you keep paying. The rate is usually variable, and cash advances typically start accruing interest on the day you withdraw the money.

Issuers typically require a minimum payment each billing cycle. Paying it avoids late fees and collection steps, but it does not stop interest on the balance that remains. Late payments can also weigh on your payment history.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
