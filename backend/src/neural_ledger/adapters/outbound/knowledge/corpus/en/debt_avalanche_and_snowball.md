The debt avalanche and the debt snowball are two ways to order repayments when you owe several debts: you pay the minimum on every debt and send all the extra money to one target.

Both methods start the same way. You keep the minimum payment on each debt so no account falls behind, then direct the surplus to a single debt until it is cleared. The amount you were paying on that debt then rolls into the next target.

The avalanche targets the debt with the highest interest rate first. On paper it usually costs the least in total interest, because the most expensive balance stops growing sooner. Its drawback is that the first debt it clears may be large, so progress can feel slow.

The snowball targets the smallest balance first, whatever its rate. Clearing small debts quickly gives early wins, which many people find motivating. It often costs more interest than the avalanche, yet some studies have found that snowball users were more likely to finish paying off their debts.

Neither method is better for everyone. The right choice depends on how you respond to visible progress, how large the rate differences are, and whether you can keep the discipline over time. In Chile the same logic applies to card balances, cuotas and bank loans, as long as you know each one's rate and minimum.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
