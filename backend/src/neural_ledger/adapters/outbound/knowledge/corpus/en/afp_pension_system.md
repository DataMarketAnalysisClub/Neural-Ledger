Chile's AFP pension system is a system of individual retirement accounts. Each worker's mandatory contributions are invested by a pension fund administrator, called an AFP, and the accumulated balance is used to finance a pension at retirement. The Superintendencia de Pensiones oversees the system.

The mandatory contribution, or cotización obligatoria, is a percentage of the worker's taxable income that is deposited each month into the personal account. The contribution rate is set by law, so the worker does not choose how much goes in, although voluntary savings such as APV are a separate option.

Savings are invested in multifunds, or multifondos. These are investment funds with different levels of risk, labelled with letters from A to E. Fund A holds the largest share of volatile assets and fund E the smallest. Affiliates may choose a multifund within the rules, and if they do not choose, they are assigned according to their age, which generally means more volatile funds for younger workers.

The main risks are that the value of the investments can fall, especially in funds with more shares, and that the final pension depends on contributions, investment results and the conditions at retirement. No return can be assumed in advance, and the pension may turn out lower than expected.

Fees and commissions charged by the AFP also affect the balance, so reviewing the official information from the Superintendencia de Pensiones is a useful habit.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
