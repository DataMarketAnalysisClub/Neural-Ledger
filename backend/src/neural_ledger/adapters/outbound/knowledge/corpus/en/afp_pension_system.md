Chile's AFP pension system is a system of individual retirement accounts. Each worker's mandatory contributions are invested by a pension fund administrator, called an AFP, and the accumulated balance is used to finance a pension at retirement. In Chile, this system is regulated by the Superintendencia de Pensiones.

The mandatory contribution, or cotización obligatoria, is a share of the worker's taxable income that is deposited each month into the personal account. The worker does not choose how much goes in, although voluntary savings such as APV are a separate option.

Savings are invested in multifunds, or multifondos: funds with different risk levels, labelled with letters from A to E. Fund A holds the largest share of shares and is the most volatile, while Fund E is the most conservative; B, C and D sit in between. Affiliates may choose their multifund, and if they do not choose, the AFP assigns one according to age, which generally means more volatile funds for younger workers.

The main risks are that the value of the investments can fall, especially in funds with more shares, and that the final pension depends on contributions, investment results and conditions at retirement. No return can be assumed in advance, and the pension may turn out lower than expected.

Fees charged by the AFP also affect the balance, so reviewing the official information from the Superintendencia de Pensiones is a useful habit.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
