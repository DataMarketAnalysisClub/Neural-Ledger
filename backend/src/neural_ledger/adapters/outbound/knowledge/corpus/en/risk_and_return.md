Risk and return describe a basic trade-off in investing: investments that can offer higher returns also carry a greater chance of losses or of results that vary widely. Investors generally expect extra return as compensation for accepting extra uncertainty.

Risk, in this sense, is how far actual outcomes may differ from what was expected, including the possibility of losing part of the amount invested. Return is the gain or loss from holding an asset, which includes income such as interest or dividends and any change in its price.

Assets can be placed along a rough spectrum. Short-term government debt is usually among the lower-risk options, while longer-term bonds, corporate debt, property, shares and cryptocurrencies show more variability. This ordering is a general tendency, not a rule: a given asset can behave differently in a given period, and riskier assets can also perform poorly for long stretches.

Thinking about this trade-off also means considering how much loss you could accept, when you will need the money, and how you would react to a fall in prices. A higher advertised return is a good prompt to ask what risk stands behind it.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
