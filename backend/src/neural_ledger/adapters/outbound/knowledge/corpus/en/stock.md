A stock is a share of ownership in a company. Owning one gives you a claim on part of the company's profits and assets, and usually a vote on certain corporate decisions.

The price of a stock moves with what buyers and sellers expect from the company and from the wider market. Some companies pay part of their profits to shareholders as dividends, but they are not obliged to, so the income from a stock can vary or stop.

The main risk is that the price can fall, sometimes sharply, and may never recover. If a company fails, shareholders are paid only after creditors such as lenders, so they often receive little or nothing. Prices can also swing for reasons unrelated to the business itself, such as news events or changes in interest rates.

In Chile, shares of companies listed on the stock exchange are bought and sold through brokers, and the CMF supervises the securities market. Holding a single stock concentrates your exposure in one company, which is why many people think about spreading their money across more than one investment.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
