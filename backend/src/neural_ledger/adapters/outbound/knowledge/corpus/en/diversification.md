Diversification means spreading money across different assets, sectors or markets so that no single holding decides the outcome of the whole portfolio. The idea is that assets which do not move in lockstep can cushion one another when some of them fall.

It works best against risk that belongs to one company or one industry. If one holding performs badly, the others can offset part of the loss. Spreading money across many holdings also reduces dependence on a single issuer or on the economy of a single country.

Its limits matter. Diversification cannot remove market-wide risk, which tends to affect nearly every holding during a crisis. Correlations between markets often rise in downturns, so protection can be weaker exactly when it is most needed. It does not raise expected returns, and it can reduce the upside when one holding does very well. Fees add up when a portfolio holds many small positions.

In Chile, many people diversify through funds that hold many assets. A fund concentrated in one sector or one country, however, offers less spread than its name may suggest, so checking what it actually holds is the practical test.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
