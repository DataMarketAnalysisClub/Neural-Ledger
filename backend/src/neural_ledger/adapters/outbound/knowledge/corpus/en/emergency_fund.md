An emergency fund is money kept apart for urgent, unexpected costs, such as losing your job, a large medical bill or a major car repair. It is not for holidays, gifts or investing. Its purpose is to keep an unplanned bill from turning into debt.

A common benchmark is to cover between 3 and 6 months of essential expenses: rent, basic food, utilities, transport to work and key health costs. Your number depends on your situation. A person with a stable indefinite contract may need the lower end, while someone with variable income, self-employed work or several dependents usually needs more.

Keep the fund somewhere you can reach quickly without losing value. Access matters more here than returns. Building it gradually, through a small automatic transfer each month, is easier than trying to save the full amount at once.

The 3-to-6-month range is a general planning rule, not a rule set by Chilean law. Start by calculating your real essential costs, because the whole estimate depends on them.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
