An emergency fund is money kept apart for urgent, unexpected costs, such as losing your job, a large medical bill or a major car repair. It is not for holidays, gifts or investing. Its purpose is to keep an unplanned bill from turning into debt.

A common benchmark is to cover between 3 and 6 months of essential expenses: rent, basic food, utilities, transport to work and key health costs. Dining out and entertainment are left out of the calculation, since they can be cut quickly when an emergency arrives. The right number depends on the situation: someone with variable income, self-employed work or several dependents usually needs more than someone with a stable indefinite contract.

The fund works best in a place that can be reached immediately without losing value, because in this role, withdrawing quickly counts for more than earning a high return. Many people fill it in stages, adding modest amounts over time until the target is close.

The 3-to-6-month range is a planning benchmark, not a rule set by Chilean law. The estimate begins with real essential costs, because everything else depends on them.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
