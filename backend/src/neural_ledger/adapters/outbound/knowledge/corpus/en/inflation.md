Inflation is a broad and lasting rise in the prices of goods and services, which means each unit of money buys less over time. It is not one price going up on its own; it is the average movement across a typical set of purchases.

In Chile, the most common measure is the Índice de Precios al Consumidor (IPC), calculated by the Instituto Nacional de Estadísticas (INE). It follows the price of a fixed basket of goods and services that represents household spending, and its monthly change is what people usually call the inflation rate. The Banco Central works with an inflation target, a reference point for how prices should evolve over the medium term. The latest figures are published on the INE and Banco Central websites, and they change every month.

Why it matters: if your money earns less than inflation, its purchasing power falls even when the balance rises. Inflation affects wages, rents, debts and savings in different ways. Some contracts in Chile, such as those expressed in UF, adjust automatically to it.

To read a price or a balance from another year, you need to adjust for inflation first. That is the idea behind real values, which the real versus nominal card explains.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
