A mutual fund is an investment vehicle that pools money from many people to buy a portfolio of assets, such as stocks, bonds or short-term instruments. Each investor holds units of the fund, and the value of each unit is recalculated from the fund's total assets.

In Chile, mutual funds (fondos mutuos) are managed by an asset management company, called an AGF, and supervised by the CMF. Professional managers make the buying and selling decisions, so the investor does not pick each individual holding.

Spreading money across many assets reduces the damage from any one of them, but it does not remove the risk of the markets the fund invests in. The value of a fund can fall, and its results depend on the manager's decisions and on conditions nobody controls. Management fees and other costs reduce what the investor keeps, and they apply whether or not the fund performs well.

Returns can come from income the fund earns, from selling units at a higher price, or from gains the fund distributes. Funds differ in what they invest in and how much risk they take, and the regulations and prospectus of each fund describe its specific strategy. Reading them is the most direct way to understand what you would be exposed to.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
