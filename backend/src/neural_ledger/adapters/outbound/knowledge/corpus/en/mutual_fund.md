A mutual fund is an investment vehicle that pools money from many investors and uses it to buy a portfolio of financial assets, such as shares, bonds or short-term instruments. Each investor holds units of the fund, and the value of each unit is recalculated every day from the fund's total assets.

In Chile, mutual funds (fondos mutuos) are run by an asset management company, usually called an AGF, and they are supervised by the CMF. The fund's assets are kept in custody by a separate entity, which adds a layer of control over the money.

A fund holds many assets, which spreads risk across them, but it does not remove the risk of the markets it invests in. The value of a fund can fall, and its results depend on the decisions of its manager and on conditions nobody controls. Management fees and other costs reduce what the investor keeps, and they apply whether or not the fund performs well.

Funds differ in what they invest in and how much risk they take. The regulations and the prospectus of each fund describe its specific strategy, and reading them is the most direct way to understand what you would be exposed to.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
