An exchange-traded fund, or ETF, is an investment fund whose units trade on a stock exchange during market hours, much like a share. Most ETFs hold a basket of assets, such as stocks or bonds, and many aim to follow an index, which gives exposure to many assets with a single purchase.

Because an ETF trades like a share, its market price can drift away from the value of the assets it holds, especially in stressed markets. The price may sit above or below that value, and the gap is not always corrected quickly.

The risks depend on what the ETF holds. Funds that use derivatives or borrowed money can amplify losses. Some ETFs follow their index imperfectly, a difference known as tracking error. Like any fund, an ETF moves with the markets it follows, and nothing protects its value from a decline.

In Chile, the availability, rules and tax treatment of ETFs depend on local regulation, which can change over time. To understand a particular product, read its official documents and check the public information published by the CMF.

This is general information, not a recommendation.
